# Option Pricing Lab

A Python library that prices European options by three independent methods (the Black–Scholes formula, Monte Carlo simulation and a Crank–Nicolson finite-difference solver), compares the methods with one another and tests the Black–Scholes model against S&P 500 option quotes.

## Motivation

Under a change of variables, the Black–Scholes equation reduces to the heat equation, which I studied in a first-year mathematical methods course. The project uses this connection to build a finite-difference pricer and verifies it against two independent methods. It also measures the convergence rate of each method, identifies the settings in which each method fails and quantifies the difference between the constant-volatility assumption of Black–Scholes and observed market prices.

## Data

The market data are S&P 500 index options (ticker `^SPX`, PM-settled `SPXW` contracts) from Yahoo Finance, accessed through [yfinance](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance). One expiry approximately 45 days ahead is used.

Yahoo data are licensed for personal use. `scripts/download_chain.py` stores raw snapshots in `data/`, which is excluded from version control, and the repository contains only figures and summary statistics derived from them.

Quotes are delayed by approximately 15 minutes and are not synchronous across strikes. Outside US market hours, bids and asks may be zero or stale, and deep out-of-the-money strikes have wide spreads. The number of quotes removed at each cleaning step is reported in [`results/summary.md`](results/summary.md).

No interest rate or dividend data are downloaded. The forward price and discount factor are estimated from put–call parity on the same quotes.

## Methods

### Black–Scholes

[`black_scholes.py`](src/optlab/black_scholes.py) computes closed-form prices and Greeks with a continuous dividend yield. The formula is exact under the model's assumptions and serves as the benchmark for the numerical methods.

### Monte Carlo

[`monte_carlo.py`](src/optlab/monte_carlo.py) samples the terminal price exactly from its lognormal distribution, so European payoffs require no time discretisation. Four estimators are implemented: plain sampling, antithetic variates, a control variate based on the discounted terminal price, and the combination of the last two. Each estimate is reported with its standard error.

### Finite differences

[`finite_difference.py`](src/optlab/finite_difference.py) transforms the Black–Scholes equation into the heat equation and solves it with the Crank–Nicolson scheme, using a banded Cholesky factorisation. The first two time steps are replaced by fully implicit half-steps (Rannacher start-up) to damp the errors caused by the non-smooth payoff, and the results from two grids are combined by Richardson extrapolation.

### Implied volatility and the smile

[`implied_vol.py`](src/optlab/implied_vol.py) computes implied volatility with Brent's method. [`smile.py`](src/optlab/smile.py) keeps a single settlement root, retains two-sided quotes with spreads below 25% of the mid price, estimates the forward from put–call parity with a Theil–Sen regression, keeps out-of-the-money options and computes implied volatility at the bid, mid and ask.

Mathematical notes for each method are in [`docs/notes/`](docs/notes/). Design decisions and the alternatives considered are recorded in [`docs/decisions.md`](docs/decisions.md).

## Evaluation

### Numerical methods

The closed-form price is the reference for all numerical errors. Monte Carlo accuracy is measured as the root-mean-square error over 100 independent runs at each path count. PDE accuracy is measured at the strike for price, delta and gamma, with the number of time steps set to one-eighth of the number of space steps. Convergence orders are least-squares slopes of log error against log grid size or log path count. A further check prices 400 random contracts across the validated parameter range.

Monte Carlo error decreases at the theoretical rate of N^(−1/2) for all four estimators. At 256,000 paths, antithetic and control variates together reduce the variance by a factor of 28.6, and the reported standard errors agree with the measured errors.

Under these settings, plain Crank–Nicolson fails to converge in gamma: the gamma error at the strike increases with grid refinement, with a fitted order of −1.0. When the ratio of the time step to the squared space step is large, the scheme damps the high-frequency error introduced by the payoff kink very slowly. Rannacher start-up restores second-order convergence in price, delta and gamma, and Richardson extrapolation raises the order of convergence of the price to 4.4. The extrapolation requires the number of Rannacher steps to remain fixed as the grid is refined.

### Market test

The benchmark is a single Black–Scholes volatility for the expiry, set to the at-the-money implied volatility. Its error is measured in volatility points within two standard deviations of the forward. A second statistic, the share of quotes whose bid–ask volatility range excludes the flat volatility, separates model error from quote noise.

On 19 October 2026 (SPXW options expiring 4 December 2026, 46 days to expiry), implied volatility decreases from 21.8% two standard deviations below the forward to 13.2% at the money and 11.7% two standard deviations above, and rises slightly beyond that point. Within this range, the flat volatility differs from market implied volatility by 2.90 points RMSE, with a maximum of 8.58, and lies outside the bid–ask range of all 77 quotes. Further below the forward the skew steepens, reaching approximately 70% implied volatility at ln(K/F) = −0.7.

The parity-implied forward of 7,745.20 is 0.385% above spot, consistent with the 3-month Treasury bill rate of 4.10% ([FRED DTB3](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DTB3), 15 October 2026) and a dividend yield of approximately 1%. The parity-implied interest rate of 2.96% has a large standard error at this maturity. Recomputing the implied volatilities with the Treasury bill rate changes them by at most 0.02 volatility points.

### Verification

The test suite contains 92 tests. Each pricer is compared with references that do not share its code: the worked example in Hull's *Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives*, numerical integration of the risk-neutral expectation, put–call parity, no-arbitrage bounds and the coverage of Monte Carlo confidence intervals. The market pipeline is tested on synthetic option chains with a known smile and deliberately corrupted quotes.

`scripts/mutation_check.py` introduces 15 known errors into a temporary copy of the code, one at a time, and confirms that the test suite detects each of them.

## Reproduction

Python 3.12 or later is required, together with an internet connection for the market data.

```bash
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate              # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/reproduce.py --update-readme
```

`reproduce.py` runs the test suite and the numerical studies, downloads an option chain if no snapshot is stored, and writes the figures and tables to `results/`. Subsequent runs reuse the stored snapshot. The option `--fresh` downloads a new snapshot, and `--skip-market` omits the market study. Snapshots should be taken during US market hours (09:30–16:00 New York time), because Yahoo frequently reports zero bids and asks at other times.

A single option can be priced with all three methods:

```bash
python scripts/price.py --spot 100 --strike 110 --maturity 1 --rate 0.04 --vol 0.25 --div-yield 0.01 --type put
```

## Limitations

- All three pricers implement the Black–Scholes model, which assumes constant volatility, no jumps and frictionless hedging. Their agreement verifies the numerical methods; the market test assesses the model itself.
- The market results come from a single snapshot of a single expiry.
- Yahoo quotes are delayed and asynchronous, and the cleaning thresholds are fixed by assumption.
- Only European exercise is supported. American options would require an early-exercise constraint.
- The heat-equation transformation is ill-conditioned when |r − q| is large relative to σ²/2. The solver is validated for volatilities of 10–60%, rates of −1% to 6% and dividend yields of 0–4%.
- When no simulated path finishes in the money, the Monte Carlo estimator returns zero with a zero standard error. Importance sampling would address this case.

## Further work

Planned extensions are daily tracking of the skew against the VIX, a volatility surface across expiries using the SVI parametrisation, and support for American exercise.
